# Anime Motion Fighter usability test

## Purpose

This test is designed to learn whether a first-time player can understand the camera relationship, choose a comfortable stance, calibrate their position, and experience one fair contact exchange without coaching. The observer should study **clarity, confidence, bodily comfort, and perceived fairness** before recording technical details.

## Test setup

Open the Fighter with `?test=1` added to the URL. The optional test panel records only local in-session events and provides an **Export Log** button. It does not send telemetry to a server. Use a device with a camera, but ask for permission only when the participant is ready.

| Scenario | Capture | Arena | Tracking | Preview |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Front camera | Front view | Facing Lens | Visible |
| B | Front or back camera | Side view | Side-Facing | Visible |
| C | Front or back camera | Side view | Side-Facing | Hidden after calibration |
| D | No camera permission | Any | Manual fallback | Not applicable |

## Moderator instruction

Give the participant only this instruction:

> Choose how you want to stand, enter the arena, calibrate, and land one clean contact. Please say out loud what you think the game is asking you to do. I will not explain the controls unless you are unable to continue.

Do not correct the participant during the first attempt. Note where they look, what they try first, when they become uncertain, and whether they understand why a movement counted or did not count.

## Questions after each scenario

Ask the participant: **What were you trying to do? What did you think happened? What felt clear? What felt unfair, awkward, or embarrassing? Would you choose this camera view again?** For the hidden-preview scenario, ask whether removing the monitor made the experience feel more private and whether they still trusted that tracking was active.

## Acceptance signals

The experience is ready for a broader test when most participants can complete the following without coaching:

1. They distinguish the physical camera choice from the arena presentation choice.
2. They understand that Side-Facing means turning their body relative to the lens, not merely selecting a side arena view.
3. They can complete calibration or understand the specific adjustment requested.
4. They can explain why a strike, guard, block, miss, or tracking pause occurred.
5. They can hide the preview without believing that camera tracking has stopped.
6. They can continue with manual controls when camera access is unavailable.

## Ethical boundaries

Do not record a participant’s camera feed or body image. Do not publish names, faces, or quotes without permission. The goal is to improve the experience, not to grade physical ability. A participant may stop at any time, and the manual path should remain available without making them feel that they failed.
